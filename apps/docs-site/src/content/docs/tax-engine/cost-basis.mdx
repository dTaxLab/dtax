## Supported methods

| Method | ID | Country | Notes |
|--------|----|---------|-------|
| First In, First Out | `FIFO` | 🌍 | Oldest lots sold first (IRS default) |
| Last In, First Out | `LIFO` | 🌍 | Must elect Specific ID (IRS) |
| Highest In, First Out | `HIFO` | 🌍 | Must elect Specific ID (IRS) |
| Specific ID | `SPECIFIC_ID` | 🌍 | Manual lot selection |
| Germany FIFO | `GERMANY_FIFO` | 🇩🇪 | §23 EStG, 1-year holding exemption |
| PMPA | `PMPA` | 🇫🇷 | Plus-value mobilière, weighted average |
| Total Average | `TOTAL_AVERAGE` | 🌍 | Average cost basis |
| UK Share Pooling | `UK_SHARE_POOLING` | 🇬🇧 | Section 104 pool + same-day and 30-day rules |
| AU CGT Discount | `AU_CGT_DISCOUNT` | 🇦🇺 | FIFO + 50% discount on long-term gains (ATO s.115) |
| CA ACB | `CA_ACB` | 🇨🇦 | Weighted average Adjusted Cost Base (CRA IT-387R2) |
| KR Virtual Asset | `KR_VIRTUAL_ASSET` | 🇰🇷 | FIFO; 22% flat rate + KRW 2.5M exemption at report layer |
| IN VDA | `IN_VDA` | 🇮🇳 | FIFO; losses not deductible (s.115BBH) |
| ES CGT | `ES_CGT` | 🇪🇸 | FIFO; tiered 19–27% rates at report layer |

## IRS note

The IRS allows **FIFO** and **Specific Identification** for crypto (Rev. Rul. 2023-14). LIFO and HIFO are technically Specific ID strategies — you must maintain lot-level records and consistently apply your method.

## Usage

```typescript
import { computeGains, compareAllMethods } from '@dtax/tax-engine';

// Single method
const result = computeGains(transactions, {
  method: 'FIFO',
  taxYear: 2024,
});

// Compare all comparable methods (excludes SPECIFIC_ID)
const comparison = compareAllMethods(transactions, 2024);
comparison.forEach(({ method, totalGain, recommendation }) => {
  console.log(`${method}: $${totalGain} ${recommendation ? '← recommended' : ''}`);
});
```

## Sales that span the one-year line

Each lot is held for its own period. A sale that takes units from lots on both sides of the one-year line is therefore not one short-term or long-term disposal: it is reported as **two lines on Form 8949**, a short-term line (Box A–C) and a long-term line (Box D–F), with the sale's proceeds and fee shared by units. The summary totals and Schedule D use the same two parts, so they agree with the form to the cent.

- Applies to FIFO, LIFO, HIFO and Specific ID, where units come from individual lots. Pooled methods have no per-lot period.
- Units with no purchase on record have no acquisition date and are reported short-term.
- A wash-sale loss is disallowed on the part that lost money, never on the part that gained.
- In results, `holdingPeriod` is `MIXED` for such a sale; each matched lot carries its own `holdingPeriod`. `termLegs(result)` returns the parts.

## Sales with no purchase on record

When a sale has no matching purchase, the unmatched units are reported with **no cost basis**, so the gain may be overstated. The report lists each coin and wallet with the units that are missing.

If you know what those units cost (for example from the cost column of a 1099-B or an account statement), use **Add known cost basis** next to the coin on the Form 8949 section of the tax page. Enter only the units that have no purchase on record; units and cost already in your records are not counted again.

- The entry is stored as an ordinary purchase tagged `opening-balance`. You can find and delete it in Transactions (search "Opening balance"); recalculate afterwards.
- If you know only whether the coins were held for more than a year, the entry gets a **stand-in acquisition date**. It only decides short or long term; Form 8949 shows `VARIOUS` for it. If part of the sales were held over a year and part not, add one entry for each, matching your 1099-B.
- A stand-in date is refused when the coin was sold or sent between that date and the first sale, because those sales would change. Enter the real purchase date in that case.
- A declared lot is a holding you already had, not a new purchase: it is never a replacement purchase for a wash sale, and under UK share pooling it joins the section 104 pool instead of being matched by the same-day or 30-day rule.
- If the coins came from another of your wallets, confirm that transfer first; otherwise the cost is counted twice.
- The report says how many declared entries its sales used and their cost, so you can check them against your statement.
